# Live browser lessons

The user prefers live interaction without baking or video export. The new
`?scene=rizal_live` lesson uses SVG, a paused GSAP timeline and the actual
`@hyperframes/core` GSAP frame adapter. It follows the browser foundation
illustrated in the supplied HyperFrames ZIP and does not run the Hyperframes
video-capture engine. Name this distinction accurately. The pinned core package's
browser-safe adapter is aliased directly because its root entry also exports
Node-only compilation tools. Test the real adapter when updating the package.

The official `hyperframes` and `hyperframes-cli` skills are installed in
`~/.codex/skills`. Apply their explainer, motion and runtime guidance: grow a
diagram as its relationships are named, register a completed paused timeline,
give the composition stable metadata, and inspect intermediate as well as final
states. This app embeds the core adapter rather than the full Hyperframes media
runtime: QWEEPS owns its interactive playhead and narration transport. Export,
video baking and baked map-camera instructions do not apply to this user's live
requirement. Keep the sourced vector maps for this diagrammatic history lesson.

The teaching method is independent of the renderer. Retain distinct learner
questions, visible changes that support an inference, recognizable objects,
short labels, continuity, deliberate camera framing, and source-backed answers.
The browser can express rich vector artwork and complex relationships; do not
reintroduce the old generic-box compiler as a quality ceiling.

Use one absolute lesson playhead. Geometry, text tracing, camera and transitions
must reconstruct on arbitrary seeks. React playback may advance that clock;
GSAP timelines and pure shape evaluators consume it. Background callbacks may
not be necessary to rebuild a previous frame. Check a forward, reverse and
repeated seek in the actual browser.

For shape replacement, align meaningful contours and parts. Smooth interpolation
does not repair a wrong source drawing. Preserve islands as separate subpaths;
use sourced coastlines for recognizable geography. The current lesson bundles
Natural Earth data and its provenance. Do not invent borders by drawing blobs.

For writing, prefer actual glyph outlines with measured or normalized path
lengths, then a deliberate fill phase. Bundle the font and its license. Fixed
dash lengths on a text node are an approximation whose behavior varies by glyph.

Test actual visible frames at working screen sizes. Check transitions, labels,
selected and focused objects, and the camera after opening the conversation.
A retained map thumbnail that overlaps the new illustration should move or
leave the focused view. The Rizal review caught both that overlap and a moving
Rizal label landing on another member of the civic network; both were revised.

Questions use the configured model and real source material. An authored lesson
with model-selected focused branches is not general AI scene generation. Label
that limitation honestly. Save time, camera, selection, region and narration
position before branching, and restore them on return. Narration clips may be
cached; the visual lesson must remain live and independently seekable.

Speech synchronization is semantic as well as temporal. Matching chapter duration
or elapsed counters does not establish that the voice is discussing the object
currently being drawn. For generated object diagrams, author spoken cues with
actual semantic node IDs. Write ONE connected explanation for the whole lesson,
with callbacks and causal transitions through its shared example. A beat is a
visual/navigation checkpoint within that argument, not a fresh spoken introduction
or a closing summary. Do not force one speech segment or sentence per card.
Generate the lesson script as one vocal performance and derive internal cues
afterward. The compiler matches exact phrases to local Whisper timestamps from
that recording and checks coverage for every beat. Beat boundaries follow the
measured speech; don't inject a fixed pause or restart the voice at each boundary.
Keep natural breaths and intentional thinking pauses within the explanation.
Regenerated audio must invalidate its alignment cache. Long requests can exceed
provider limits; report that explicitly and retain the previous reviewed version
instead of silently returning to independent per-beat performances.
Objects can be revisited, grouped, or shown as supporting context; they need not
be explained in a rigid one-object-at-a-time order. A context-only cue should not
falsely highlight an object as though it were individually being discussed.
While narrated, the media playhead is the visual clock. Pause, rewind and branch
return seek that same track. For continuous geometry or simulations, use suitable
measured action/word cues rather than forcing every presentation into cards.
Do not stretch unrelated visual reveals across a paragraph or guess word times
from total word count. Test at least three distinct spoken referents, including
rewind; a clock-offset check alone misses the Apple/Orange/Banana failure.
Keep each completed cue mapping as a checkpoint. A text-only mapping request has
all its source data already; if a coding agent starts using shell or browsing
tools, stop and report the failed request instead of letting an unrelated tool
loop run indefinitely. Preserve the prior reviewed lesson while an upgrade fails.
Separate observed CLI behavior and timeouts from claims about provider overload
or quota: those causes need provider evidence.

The generated browser pipeline is separate from the authored Rizal route.
`browser_lessons.py` directs source-grounded JSON into `GeneratedStage.tsx`:
comparison, process, matrix, concept, timeline, loci and diagram representations using a
curated asset catalog. Use that pipeline for new topic/PDF requests instead of
adding a hard-coded scene per module. Preserve useful descriptions, exact source
pages and qualifications, a trackable worked example and distinct inferences.
Do not claim unsupported new geometry or simulations. Inspect actual browser
frames before publishing; source or schema validation cannot establish visual
quality. Missing appendices and prerequisites remain missing, not invented.
Source fidelity is a separate gate from visual approval: check qualifications in
visible copy, narration and experiment feedback, not just the diagram's layout.
An attractive, readable diagram can still teach an unsupported assertion.
Follow-up answers consult relevant source passages beyond the visible text.
Generated branches must retain the complete parent lesson and experiment choices
as well as its visual/audio position. Provider failures must remain visible.

For spatial mechanisms or quantitative arguments, the diagram representation
supports freely positioned semantic groups, bounded SVG primitives, equations
as Unicode text, and translation/scale/opacity triggered by measured object cues.
Use it when motion or geometry carries an inference that cards cannot show.
Imported icons remain preferable for recognizable real objects; primitive
construction suits abstract vectors, waves and mathematical relations. Review
intermediate motion, label positions, finished states and reverse seeks. These
are illustrative diagrams, not a numerical physics engine. Choice buttons can
highlight and explain a case; do not promise unavailable live sliders or computed
data. Extend reusable renderer capabilities when needed, rather than asserting
that the topic itself is unsupported.

A general topic without an attached PDF still needs material factual review.
Check definitions, units, necessary versus sufficient conditions, frame-dependent
quantities and the limits of schematics or analogies. Label model-only factual
review honestly; it does not establish external source verification. When testing
the generator, fix prompts, validation, review or renderer capabilities and
regenerate through QWEEPS. Do not hand-edit its output and present it as a success
of unattended generation.

A generated branch receives `pausedContext.parentScene`: the actual visible beat's
semantic nodes, geometry, presentation, roles, shared example and representation.
Use this to retain identities and colour meanings while choosing the branch's own
teaching method. Selected IDs and a time alone do not describe the selected object.
During a spoken bridge this context must describe the previous diagram still on
screen, not merely the incoming narration beat.

Factual errors may be geometric: an arrow pointing in the wrong direction, a
curve inconsistent with the stated forces, or motion violating the conserved
quantity. The factual-repair path must allow a targeted redraw of affected
diagram beats, preserving unflagged beats and already-corrected facts, then repeat
factual and visual review. Changing only narration or labels cannot repair a
contradictory construction. Text-only correction remains appropriate for
qualifications and wording in descriptive infographic beats.
