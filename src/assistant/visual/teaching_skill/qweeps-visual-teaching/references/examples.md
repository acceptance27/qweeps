## Supplement: HyperFrames Rizal descriptive infographic

Reference ID: `hyperframes_rizal`. The user approved the Rizal component in the
later scientific-animation ZIP and explicitly permits copying its storyboard.
Its useful progression is origins/medical calling → European journey and printing
→ two novels → La Liga and work in Dapitan → trial/farewell/execution. The live
adaptation retains a short legacy chapter afterward.

The method combines descriptive headings, dates, short accounts of what happened,
recognizable illustrations and stroke construction. It tells an unfamiliar viewer
what each object represents and why it matters. A dossier groups context with its
object; a geographic journey keeps stops attached to their locations; a book
comparison keeps each publication's date and explanation beside it. Motion guides
reading instead of making the viewer guess missing definitions.

Reuse this method where it fits, rather than making every future lesson a set of
three cards. Replace the reference's invented coastlines with sourced geometry,
and verify historical claims rather than inheriting precise counts or anecdotes.
Read `assets-and-strokes.md` for its anchored draw/fill technique and the observed
normalized-stroke rounding failure.

## A* Pathfinding & Heuristics

**What progresses:** establish S and G → show travel-cost-only expansion → draw a
candidate-to-goal distance and its horizontal/vertical components → combine
travelled and remaining cost to compare candidates → change the movement rules
and examine the estimate's assumptions.

**Why this teaches:** the learner sees what the baseline lacks before adding h.
The triangle gives meaning to the square root; g and h have distinct spatial
roles before f combines them. Moving a node changes the numbers and the priority,
so the learner can investigate a counterfactual. A change of movement metric
creates a new question rather than another repeated expansion.

**Transfer:** for an ALU, establish inputs/output, show competing operations,
trace a control-selected signal, then change the operation while retaining the
same circuit. For NPC decisions, show the observed state and competing choices,
make a decision criterion visible, and change an actual input to explain the
changed choice. These are possible structures, not hard-coded content.

**Do not copy blindly:** the authored distance sandbox has direct-edge g costs;
it is not an obstacle-aware search trace. Fixed example rankings or coordinate
values must not override live values. Dijkstra is a valid baseline, not inherently
flawed. State movement-cost assumptions for admissibility.

## PDF Explainer: Chapter 2 Entrepreneurs & Competencies

**What progresses:** a triad of perspectives → a past/today comparison → traits
and a risk curve → an assessment/decision/feedback loop → a competency quadrant →
decision styles and a synthesis. The camera moves across an organized spatial
blackboard rather than presenting an unrelated full-screen card for every item.

**Why this teaches:** the representation changes with the kind of relationship.
A triad shows perspectives converging, paired columns reveal contrasts, a curve
shows how a quantity relates to another, a loop shows feedback, and quadrants
organize two dimensions. Camera navigation follows those conceptual regions.

**Transfer:** for a long source document, identify its major relationships and
connect them into an intelligible visual journey. Choose a representation for
each relationship; do not animate every paragraph or force all content into the
same diagram. Maintain enough spatial continuity to compare and revisit.

**Remaining clarity gaps:** the current Canvas reference uses fixed trait scores
and hand-shaped success curves. Its risk graph labels success probability, but
the plotted values come from display formulas, not demonstrated measurements.
Treat those shapes as illustrations, not evidence that feedback guarantees success.
The competency layout also needs a concrete case and explicit organizing dimensions
to do more than arrange four headings. Preserve the useful visual vocabulary while
improving the inferential bridge between views.

**Do not copy blindly:** the ZIP contains an authored interpretation and page
references, not the original 53-page PDF. Its people, claims, dates and page
numbers are not verified source material for another explanation. Its layout,
section count and closing maxim are not universal lesson requirements.

## Math 01: Neural Net & Loss Curve

**What progresses:** a question becomes a small persistent header → construct a
network → move sample pixels into inputs while a sample counter changes → show
activation moving through the layers → focus on a changing loss curve → transform
the measure to accuracy and show its growth → emphasize the result.

**Why this teaches:** construction introduces parts before activity. Samples and
activations give the network an observable mechanism. The graph connects those
local operations to a measured outcome. Transforming the existing readout and
curve preserves their conceptual relationship, instead of replacing them with
a disconnected claim that learning happened.

**Transfer:** in a world-generator explanation, follow an actual seed through
intermediate representations and show how a parameter changes the terrain. In an
ALU, carry the same input bits through a circuit and connect the selected path to
the output. In Python, step through one concrete program while variable state
changes, then generalize the language concept from that example.

**Do not copy blindly:** 97.4%, sample counts and curve shapes are illustrative
values in this reference. They are not measurements for a new model. The number
of layers or animation beats should follow the subject and learning objective.
