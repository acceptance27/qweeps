# Memory cues and kinetic explanation

The user proposed method of loci, dual coding, kinetic infographics and visual
mnemonics after approving the descriptive live Rizal lesson. Add them to the
available methods without replacing the accepted presentation or forcing a
memory palace onto every subject. Distinguish understanding a relationship from
remembering its names, order or rules; a lesson can serve both goals.

## Pick a technique for the job

- **Method of loci:** useful for recalling an ordered collection. Plan a stable
  spatial route with distinct landmarks and place a clear cue at each location.
  Revisit the same places rather than moving their meanings around. A row of
  cards or numbered chapters alone does not establish a memory palace. Include
  an opportunity to mentally retrace the route and retrieve its associated facts.
- **Dual coding:** pair meaningful imagery with nearby verbal identification or
  explanation. The picture and words should refer to the same concept and add
  useful information. Keep the descriptions that make an unfamiliar infographic
  comprehensible. With narration, avoid competing changes and needless verbatim
  duplication; with sound off, the visible explanation must still work.
- **Kinetic infographic:** direct attention through a readable sequence: establish
  the entity, describe it, enact the relationship and reveal its consequence.
  Use anchored strokes, staged labels, shape continuity and occasional emphasis
  where they help. An elastic pop-in is a stylistic option, not a requirement.
  Motion must not imply historical causation or measured influence without evidence.
- **Visual mnemonic:** for an unfamiliar term, sequence or rule, choose an
  interpretable cue and state what it stands for. Interaction between objects can
  carry the relation; a static decorative icon cannot substitute for its definition.
  Keyword and shape mnemonics can fit vocabulary or symbols. Vivid or unusual
  imagery is optional: choose associations that fit the learner and the subject.

In `representation`, explain which techniques serve understanding and which
serve recall. For a mnemonic, record the cue → intended fact mapping and how
the learner will recover the meaning. A recall check can go in a beat's
`interaction`: hide the verbal answer, revisit a cue, ask for its meaning, then
reveal the explanation. Do not claim a recall check is interactive unless the
chosen renderer actually implements it.

For Rizal, potential cues are study/book → early learning, route → European
study, two novels → political writing, water channel → civic work in exile,
farewell document → execution and surviving writing, later readers → public
memory. These can retain stable positions and names. They are retrieval cues,
not replacements for dates, descriptions or sources. Do not invent symbolic
historical objects and present them as things Rizal actually owned or used.

## Review and evidence

Check the complete presentation for comprehension, then test whether the cue
retrieves the intended meaning without introducing a plausible wrong one.
Observe recall or ask the learner rather than declaring that an animation has
proven retention. Preserve accurate geometry, useful descriptions and the
ability to pause, inspect and return from a question.

Do not promise “double recall,” automatic storage in two independent memories,
or universal improvement. Paivio's verbal/nonverbal representation account and
Mayer's multimedia-learning model are related but not identical. Printed words
are visually presented; naming two channels does not remove capacity limits.
Memory-palace research involves deliberate practice, not merely viewing rooms.

Primary research and theory:

- [Dresler et al., 2017, mnemonic training](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5439266/): method-of-loci training and subsequent memory performance.
- [Mayer and Moreno, 2002, multimedia learning](https://www.sfu.ca/~jcnesbit/EDUC220/ThinkPaper/MayerMoreno2002.pdf): complementary words/pictures, contiguity and cognitive-load considerations.
- [Mayer, 2024, theory review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-023-09842-1): dual channels, limited capacity and active processing.
